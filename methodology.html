Read before using

How to use this repository

This MVP follows practical research-repository principles: traceability, clear metadata, controlled categories, transparent uncertainty, and no unsupported inference.

1. Start with a question

Use the source catalogue filters to locate material by system, geography, methodology, or theme. Read the original record before relying on a summary.

2. Read the scope

Each source detail page preserves population, geography, sample size, and method where recorded. Local findings should not be treated as national estimates.

3. Separate records from synthesis

Recorded findings are paraphrases entered by the team in the source catalogue. They are not a new independent study, meta-analysis, or causal estimate.

4. Use numbers cautiously

Implementation figures are reported as source data. Definitions of “identified,” “eligible,” “rehabilitated,” and “recipient” may differ across records and years.

5. Treat gaps as information

Partial records, missing source links, unmatched denominators, and thin geographic coverage are shown openly. They should guide future verification and research.

6. Cite the original source

Use this hub to discover and navigate sources. Cite the original report, paper, law, or dataset—not the repository—when making a substantive claim.

What this MVP includes

A curated evidence catalogue, not a systematic review.

Professional evidence maps normally require a published search protocol, explicit screening rules, dual checking, and a reproducible update process. The team’s material is not complete enough to claim that standard.

Included now

  • All populated source-catalogue records, including partial entries
  • Searchable source metadata and recorded findings
  • Separate policy and publication timelines
  • District-level Kamaiya implementation display
  • Source detail pages and direct links where recorded

Deliberately not included

  • A citation network: source-to-source citation edges have not been verified
  • A claim map presented as a systematic synthesis
  • Percentages derived from incompatible denominators
  • National conclusions from district-specific research
  • Predictions, rankings, or causal estimates

Repository limitations

What this evidence hub cannot establish

Transparency about coverage and method is essential. Use the catalogue to locate material, then verify claims against the original source and its context.

Language and search coverage

Search and screening were conducted in English. Nepali-language literature and materials published in other languages may be absent.

Not a systematic review

The catalogue is curated from the team’s working materials. It does not claim exhaustive searching, duplicate screening, or a reproducible systematic-review protocol.

Access barriers

Records behind paywalls, in subscription journals, or without stable public links may not be represented, even where they are relevant.

Uneven source detail

Metadata, geographic specificity, samples, dates and original links vary by source. Missing fields do not prove that a source contains no information.

No pooled estimates

Definitions, denominators, dates and study populations differ. The repository does not combine them into comparative rates or national totals.

Maps show coverage, not prevalence

Geographic highlighting indicates reported coverage in repository materials. It is not a prevalence map and should not be read as evidence of absence elsewhere.

Recommended next update

Move from catalogue to living evidence base.

Verify links and bibliographies

Resolve all partial records, add DOI/stable URLs, and capture cited references from the full texts before building any citation network.

Code evidence consistently

Use a shared codebook for claims, populations, geography, dates, and evidence strength; preserve page-level locators for each finding.
